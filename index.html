Research Lab

Find why a country is not productive. Trust data, not people.

Step 0: Know your starting point

Write this before you research. At the end you will compare. Most people only look for facts that agree with what they already think. This stops that.

Step 1: Define "not productive"

Pick a number you can measure. Examples: output per worker, income per person (GDP per capita), growth rate over 20 years, share of exports that are processed goods.

Step 2: Choose comparison countries

A country is only "low" compared to others. Choose 4:

  • 2 that started near the same level 30 to 50 years ago and did better.
  • 1 that started the same and did about the same or worse.
  • 1 close neighbour (same region, climate, history).
Why include one that did worse or the same? If you only pick winners, you will only find reasons that fit the winners.

The full method

  1. Guide: steps 0 to 2 above.
  2. Facts: collect numbers. Each number needs unit, year, source.
  3. Causes: test all 10 areas. Look for evidence against each one, not only for it.
  4. Check: test every claim you read before using it.
  5. Result: rank the causes, write your conclusion, then test it.

Facts

Save every number you find. No source, no year, no unit means the number does not count.

Free data sources

Rule: if two sources give different numbers, write both and find out why (different year, unit, or method). Do not choose the one you like.

Test the 10 areas

Open each area. Check the list. Write evidence for and against. Then give a strength.

Strength "Strong" needs all 3: (1) two independent sources, (2) your country is clearly worse than the comparison countries, (3) you looked for evidence against it and it did not break the idea.

Check a claim

Use this on every article, report, video, or answer from any AI, including this app's maker. Tick only what you really did.

Mistakes that give wrong answers

Using AI for research

  • Ask it for sources and search words, not for the answer.
  • Open every source it gives. AI can invent sources or numbers.
  • Ask it to argue the opposite of your idea, then check that too.
  • No single source is final: not an AI, not a government, not a university, not an international agency. Agreement from independent sources is what counts.

My conclusion

If you are more sure than before and you found little evidence against, be careful. Show your work to 2 people who disagree with you and ask them to break it.
